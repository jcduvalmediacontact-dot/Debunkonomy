The argument in ten points

The intellectual backbone of the book, formulated for the curious mind and the impatient reader.

  1. 01The contemporary ecological crisis is not an environmental crisis — it is a monetary crisis.
  2. 02Money is not a neutral tool. It is the invisible language of power: it determines what exists and what is condemned to invisibility.
  3. 03Modern money is born from private bank debt, which structurally imposes on every actor a short-term profitability constraint — and therefore an extraction imperative.
  4. 04Green growth is a chimera. The decoupling of economic growth from ecological externalities is empirically impossible.
  5. 05Certain activities will have to shrink. Degrowth is not impoverishment: it is liberation from financial servitude.
  6. 06The problem will not be solved by microeconomic innovation or individual virtue. It demands a macroeconomic mega-innovation.
  7. 07NEMO IMS proposes inverting the monetary polarity: a currency created without debt, anchored to the measurable regeneration of the living world.
  8. 08Alongside the "yang" finance of markets, a yin finance must be established, dedicated to the regeneration of planetary commons.
  9. 09NEMO money is created upstream of certified regenerative activities and destroyed at each market transaction via a weighted melt (demurrage) calibrated to ecological impact.
  10. 10NEMO IMS is not merely a technical reform: it is a peaceful revolution, grounded in a new understanding of money as the thermodynamics of the living world.

Detailed contents

Ten chapters, 269 pages. From the critique of the debt-money system to the complete architecture of NEMO IMS.

Intro

Why The Economy of Balance?

Sets the diagnosis: the ecological crisis is first and foremost monetary. Introduces the taboo word — degrowth — and distinguishes it from its euphemisms (transition, sobriety, green growth). Presents NEMO IMS as a refoundation of the monetary system rather than yet another reform.

Ch. 1

Why Change Our Economy?

An opening metaphor to understand NEMO IMS. The principle of dual Yin/Yang finance. The three fundamental pillars of robustness, set against the three dogmas of the dominant economy.

A Metaphor to Understand NEMO IMS · Liberating the Living World from Financial Servitude · The Three Pillars of Robustness

Ch. 2

The Dilemmas of Our Time

Case studies: fast fashion, extractive industries, the contradictions of the energy transition. Why conventional responses are structurally incapable of solving structural crises.

Ch. 3

Histories and Futures of Money

The most substantial chapter in the book. How we moved from money-as-tool to money-as-debt. A mapping of the major monetary theories (neoclassical, post-Keynesian, institutionalist, MMT) and their ecological blind spots.

A Degenerative Paradigm · Cross-Perspectives and Ideologies · Dilemmas Without Money, Dilemmas With Money

Ch. 4

Towards a New Ethics of Value

The building metaphor: we demolish the foundations to add new floors in homage to GDP. A critique of economic value that ignores "the things we never count."

Ch. 5

The Monetary Curses

Three structural paradoxes of the current financial system: bank money creation mechanically manufactures extraction; profitability and regeneration are incompatible; financial debts and planetary debts form a Gordian knot.

Ch. 6

What If the Planet Had Its Own Bank?

The diagnosis of an obsolete economic system, and the outline of a monetary institution dedicated to financing the regeneration of the living world. The "Sisyphean curse" of public debt.

Ch. 7

The GAÏA Economic Symposium

The architectural heart of the proposal. A global governance of planetary commons, and the seven revolutions that accompany it: economic, monetary, financial, of work and merit, legal, accounting, banking.

Ch. 8

International Trade with NEMO IMS

An exchange standard based on ecological impact rather than on power relations between currencies. Gradually breaking free from dependence on hegemonic currencies without replacing them with another hegemony.

Ch. 9

NEMO IMS Macroprudential Recommendations

The technical mechanics. A monetary thermodynamics, the "mixer" metaphor between hot money (bank, extractive) and cold money (regenerative). Required reserves, differentiated melt rates, NEMO Green SDRs.

Ch. 10

Rethinking the Economy and Remaking the World

Three scenarios for the advent of NEMO IMS: transformation through global institutions (IMF, BIS, UN); a coalition of pioneer states, modeled on the euro or the BRICS; democratic pressure carried by ecological political movements.

Chapter 10

NEMO Glossary

Fifteen key concepts from the book, for readers and for the machines that strive to understand.

NEMO IMS

NEgentropic MOney International Monetary System. A proposal to refound the international monetary system in which money creation is dis-embedded from debt and anchored to the measurable regeneration of the living world.

Monetary negentropy

The transposition to the monetary field of the thermodynamic concept of negentropy. A negentropic currency is issued as the counterpart of activities that restore organised complexity in the living world — as opposed to an entropic currency that catalyses the dissipation of resources.

Yin / Yang Finance

Yang finance designates classical market finance. Yin finance designates a finance dedicated to the regeneration and preservation of planetary commons, financing what markets structurally ignore.

Weighted monetary melt (demurrage)

A mechanism for the progressive destruction of money during transactions, with a variable rate according to the ecological impact of the activity. The equivalent of a generalised ecological VAT operating at the heart of the monetary system.

NEMO Green SDRs

An instrument inspired by IMF Special Drawing Rights, dedicated to financing certified regenerative activities. Issued without debt, conditional on the achievement of regeneration objectives.

GAÏA Economic Symposium

A proposed global institution to govern planetary commons. It defines the labels for regenerative activities, monetary melt rates, and the major macroprudential balances of the system.

Impossible decoupling

The central empirical thesis: it is structurally impossible to grow the economy without increasing ecological externalities. Green growth is therefore an oxymoron.

Ecosystemic robustness

The opposite of microeconomic profitability. The capacity of a system to durably preserve the conditions of life. The guiding principle that must replace the maximisation of profit.

Regenerative activity

An activity whose net result is the improvement of a planetary common (reforestation, decontamination, biodiversity, food security). Insolvent in the current system because non-market in the short term.

Degenerative activity

An activity whose net result is the degradation of a planetary common. Often the most profitable in the current system — hence the structural lock-in.

The monetary curses

Three structural paradoxes: private bank money creation manufactures extraction; bank money makes decoupling impossible; financial debts and planetary debts form a Gordian knot.

Monetary thermodynamics

An approach that treats money as an energy flow subject to laws of conservation and dissipation, rather than as a simple accounting stock. The theoretical foundation of NEMO IMS macroprudential tools.

Doughnut theory

A reference to Kate Raworth. The space of economic habitability: between a floor of fundamental human needs and a ceiling of planetary limits. NEMO IMS conceives itself as the monetary architecture of this space.

Planetary debt

The cumulative gap between human withdrawals from ecosystems and their regenerative capacity. Invisible in classical accounting; structural in NEMO accounting.

Monetary alpha

The hegemonic currency of a given monetary order (dollar, potentially yuan). Changing the alpha does not change the paradigm: what is needed is a change of system.
